# Typesafe for advertising decisions: ads and ad-set profitability

Research and evaluation protocol, 2026-09-21. This work evaluates recommendations; it does not change advertising delivery, budgets, approvals, or normal-session behavior. A successful API call, correct synthetic label, or schema-valid recommendation is not proof that pausing an ad increases profit.

## Where the advantage could be

The most credible benefit is inexpensive, repeated **semantic analysis alongside ordinary calculations**, not replacing arithmetic or Meta's delivery optimizer.

| Candidate                    | Typesafe's contribution                                                                                                                             | Evidence needed before relying on it                                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ad-to-landing-page alignment | Identify an unsupported promise, wrong offer, missing eligibility condition, or contradictory CTA.                                                  | Independently labelled real ad/page pairs; mismatch precision, missed problems, and reviewer time.         |
| Recommendation review        | Detect claims that confuse clicks with purchases, ROAS with profit, incomplete inventories with exhaustive rankings, or confidence with permission. | False acceptance of unsafe reasoning, abstention coverage, and comparison with the current review process. |
| Creative learning            | Label hook, angle, offer, proof, format, awareness stage, and funnel fit consistently across many ads.                                              | Label accuracy, total extraction/analysis cost, and whether the labels improve useful decisions.           |
| Individual-ad selection      | Classify evidence against a stated policy, with more than one qualifying ad and explicit insufficient-evidence outcomes.                            | Correct policy application first; later, a controlled comparison of business outcomes.                     |
| Predictive creative features | Add semantic features to an outcome model rather than ask for an invented probability of profit.                                                    | Out-of-time, campaign/creative-family-separated evaluation against the same model without those features.  |

Typesafe's [official advertising examples](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/use-case-map) cover suitability, prohibited claims, creative quality, and landing-page alignment. These are suggested uses, not advertising-uplift results. Its [feature-discovery cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/autoresearch_feature_discovery) demonstrates semantic features feeding a supervised model on wine reviews; that supports an experimental pattern, not an advertising performance claim.

Jev 1.13 is [text-only](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models). Video, image, and audio work needs a separate extraction step. Evaluate that step's errors and cost as part of the workflow; do not describe a text-only score as direct visual inspection. The vendor explicitly says [arithmetic and date comparisons belong in code](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13), and warns about adversarial content and inconsistent relationships between separate judgments.

## Public advertising use: what is actually established

The widely shared [StealAds demo by Matthew Berman](https://x.com/TheMattBerman/status/2100654891756589230) reports bulk tagging of 724 ads from 37 brands by hook, offer, CTA, awareness stage, and landing-page mismatch. The original post was inaccessible to this research client; public reproductions of its speed/cost figures are **author-reported claims**, not independently reproduced results or total-pipeline accounting. It is not evidence that Typesafe identifies profitable ads or should pause them.

An inspectable [Five to Nine project issue](https://github.com/Nishfleet/0509/issues/3606) proposes ad/page consistency checks, advisory badges, and calibration before gating. The issue was open when inspected: useful design evidence, not a completed implementation or effectiveness study. This research found no verified public controlled study showing Typesafe improves keep/pause decisions or advertising profit.

## First advertising suite: 48 synthetic cases

The `advertising` suite extends the existing evaluation harness rather than the production advertising workflow. Its [corpus](../config/scripts/typesafe-advertising-evaluation-corpus.mjs) contains 16 authored cases for each task, each split into four development and 12 holdout cases. Related paraphrases remain within a split. Expected labels and rationales are evaluator-side, not model inputs.

| Task                      | What is checked                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Comparator and limitations                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ad_sibling_selection`    | Three ads in the same fictional ad set; multiple keepers, weak outcomes, insufficient spend, missing metrics, incomplete inventory, stale timestamps, inconsistent hierarchy/windows, paused parents, last-active protection, and injected ad names. | Typesafe and Gemini 3.8 Flash versus a deterministic numerical reference. This tests fidelity to a fictional purchase policy, not selection of real profitable ads. |
| `ad_creative_alignment`   | Supported offers, contradictions, missing/stale terms, material omissions, paraphrases, and instructions embedded in ad/page text.                                                                                                                   | Typesafe and Gemini on identical text. Human-authored synthetic labels are not independent real-world ground truth or a legal compliance judgment.                  |
| `ad_recommendation_audit` | Supported descriptions versus invalid inferences, missing outcomes, attribution conflicts, fabricated approval, and misleading reasoning.                                                                                                            | Typesafe and Gemini on the same evidence. A supported explanation does not establish authority or causal benefit.                                                   |

The [numerical reference](../config/scripts/typesafe-advertising-numeric-reference.mjs) recomputes the fictional rule from evidence rather than trusting the model's verdict. It is **not the deployed advertising optimizer**. Its thresholds are fixture design, not recommended account settings. Code computes spend/return ratios, time freshness, scope, and eligibility flags; models receive those computed flags. Correctly repeating them may demonstrate reliable policy interpretation, but cannot demonstrate additional predictive intelligence. Numerical-only work should remain in code when it already solves the problem.

The shared [evaluation protocol](typesafe-evaluation-protocol.md) governs provider pins, split discipline, failure accounting, counterbalancing, sanitized artifacts, and GitHub-only execution. Offline regressions run through required repository checks; live inference is explicitly dispatched on the checked `main-next` commit. No Mac/fleet suite, automatic paid schedule, legacy scheduler, or real ad mutation is introduced. Record results only after observing the actual run; this document declares no advertising accuracy, speedup, or savings result.

## Rich context and pairwise comparison: separate implementation

A separate [48-case context/pairwise corpus](../config/scripts/typesafe-advertising-pairwise-corpus.mjs) and [numerical reference](../config/scripts/typesafe-advertising-pairwise-reference.mjs) are implemented as evaluation source, not a live result. There are **12 scenario families**, three development and nine holdout, each expanded into `current_only`/`with_baselines` context and `AB`/`BA` displayed-ad order. That is 48 observations per model, not 48 independent advertising situations. Stable physical ad IDs are remapped to slots; experiment metadata stays evaluator-side.

- **Context ablation:** the implemented `with_baselines` condition adds time-available 14-day metrics for both ads and the ad-set aggregate; `current_only` omits those fields entirely. Objective, decision time, model, criteria, and answer space stay fixed. Only two families predeclare legitimate movement from insufficient to determinate evidence; adequate current evidence remains authoritative in the other cases. Longer context is not presumed better.
- **Pairwise answers:** the implemented Choice vocabulary is `KEEP_A`, `KEEP_B`, `KEEP_BOTH`, `KEEP_NEITHER`, and `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`. This means fictional policy eligibility, not predicted profit. Never force one winner where both ads qualify or evidence cannot distinguish them.
- **Position bias:** repeat each content pair with A/B positions reversed and remap answers to stable ad IDs. Report disagreement after remapping. `KEEP_BOTH`, `KEEP_NEITHER`, and `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` must remain invariant. A repeated reordered pair is not a new independent quality sample.
- **Nontransitivity:** do not assume A over B and B over C imply A over C. Track contradictory pairwise cycles and defer ranking when unresolved; a tournament score alone does not establish a coherent business ordering.
- **Sequencing:** family index alternates which context comes first, so six families start `current_only` and six start `with_baselines`. `floor(index/2)` alternates the displayed-ad order presented first, so six families start AB and six start BA; each combined first-context/first-position ordering occurs in three families. Every family still has all four variants. Separately, the shared runner alternates Typesafe/Gemini provider-call order by case. These are distinct counterbalances, not a claim that every context comparison follows a temporal ABBA schedule.
- **Latency and cost:** compare matched context variants within each provider, and matched provider observations within each variant. Report call counts, failures, tokens, full cost-coverage denominators, median and descriptive tails. Counterbalancing reduces order bias but does not by itself prove a causal latency improvement from extra context; service noise, payload size, and correlated cases remain. Do not mix arms, zero-fill missing usage, or label token savings as media-spend savings.
- **Small denominators:** group comparisons by underlying ad set/scenario family, not by API request. Report failed/missing observations. No automatic promotion from an improved synthetic percentage.

Richer context should explain a decision, not quietly disclose its answer. A deterministic baseline's keep/pause verdict must not be included in a candidate's prompt when testing independent selection. When intentionally supplied as an audit target, label the task as **reviewing a baseline** and score it separately.

## Priority: identify profitable ad sets early, without invented certainty

The [ad-set profitability corpus](../config/scripts/typesafe-adset-profitability-corpus.mjs) and [seeded sequential simulation](../config/scripts/adset-profitability-simulation.mjs) now exist as **evaluation source; execution results are not established by this document**. This extends the question from “which sibling ad satisfies a rule?” to “which ad set has enough evidence of positive contribution, and how soon can we responsibly say so?” The model must not manufacture a profit probability from its own Choice confidence.

The provider corpus has **16 scenario families with three related variants/rotations each**: four development families (12 snapshots) and 12 holdout families (36 snapshots). Each snapshot contains three candidate ad sets; neither the 48 snapshots nor the candidate count is an independent real-world sample size. Its two questions independently report `winner` (`ADSET_A`, `ADSET_B`, `ADSET_C`, `TIE`, or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`) and `readiness` (`OBSERVE` or `READY_FOR_HUMAN_REVIEW`). A unique relative winner may still be unlikely to profit. Review readiness additionally requires every evidence guard, the declared posterior threshold, and a positive simultaneous sequential lower bound. This is rule interpretation over code-produced statistics, not a live profit predictor. No label authorizes execution.

Define the target before ranking: positive expected contribution at a specified future spend level, highest expected contribution dollars, highest probability of being positive, and best among candidates are different objectives. A tiny but reliable opportunity can have a higher probability of positive contribution than a larger, less certain opportunity. Allow several profitable candidates, none, and insufficient evidence.

### What profit means in the experiment

Revenue minus advertising spend is not net profit. [Contribution margin](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/3-1-explain-contribution-margin-and-calculate-contribution-margin-per-unit-contribution-margin-ratio-and-total-contribution-margin) subtracts variable costs before fixed costs and operating profit. The evaluation must name whether its measure includes product/fulfilment costs, transaction fees, refunds, servicing costs, taxes, overhead, or estimated future lifetime value. Do not subtract advertising twice if it is already included in the margin input.

For the simplified simulation, let `m` be known constant contribution per conversion **before media cost**, `c` known constant cost per eligible click, and `p` the conversion probability. Expected contribution after media per click is `p × m − c`; the break-even conversion rate is `c / m` when `m > 0`. This is conditional expected contribution, not realized future profit, incremental advertising profit, or whole-business net income. Unknown margins, uncertain refunds, changing product mix, or uncertain attribution block that simple claim or require explicitly modelled uncertainty. Cheap leads alone cannot establish customer contribution.

### Conditional posterior, not a guarantee

With a `Beta(a,b)` prior and `s` conversions in `n` eligible, mature Bernoulli trials, the posterior is `Beta(a+s,b+n−s)`. Code—not Typesafe—computes `P(p > c/m | observed data, model)` and any equal-tail credible interval. The [Stan case study](https://mc-stan.org/learn-stan/case-studies/pool-binary-trials.html) explains the Beta/binomial model; [NIST's incomplete-beta identity](https://dlmf.nist.gov/8.17.E5) supports the integer-parameter calculation.

A displayed 95% or 99% posterior probability depends on the chosen prior, likelihood, trial definition, stable rate, complete outcomes, and known costs. It is **not** a 95%/99% promise that a production decision will profit, or a frequentist guarantee across repeated looks and many ad sets. Conversions must match the trial denominator: repeated purchases, view-through attribution, fractional modelled events, or more conversions than clicks do not fit this simple binary-per-click model. Prior sensitivity and model misspecification are part of the test, not implementation trivia.

### Predeclared seeded simulation

The numerical study defaults to 500 synthetic campaigns per scenario, three ad sets per campaign, seed `20260921`, and looks at 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800 **delivered** clicks per ad set. Eight scenarios therefore represent 4,000 campaign replications and 12,000 ad-set paths, not live campaigns. All seven arms use the same paired paths. The lag scenario withholds the latest 50 clicks from the mature evidence cohort while decision-time spend includes all delivered clicks across the three ad sets. The reference costs are `c = $2`, `m = $40`, so break-even is 5%. These values are simulator settings, not Sal's targets or permission to spend. Keep the scenario order fixed when comparing seeded reruns because scenarios consume a shared deterministic random stream.

| Arm                            | Decision evidence                                                                                               | Interpretation                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Observed CPA/ROAS reference    | At least 50 mature clicks; a positive claim additionally requires at least three conversions.                   | Simple baseline; does not quantify sequential uncertainty.                                                                                                               |
| `Beta(1,1)` posterior          | Separate 95% and 99% positive/negative posterior thresholds.                                                    | Model-conditional Bayesian decisions; measure repeated-look errors empirically.                                                                                          |
| Optimistic `Beta(20,80)` prior | Same thresholds, intentionally misspecified for some scenarios.                                                 | Exposes early overconfidence from unsuitable historical priors.                                                                                                          |
| Sequential Hoeffding reference | Two-sided bound with per-look error allocation `δₖ = 0.05 / (3k(k+1))`, `k ≥ 1`; radius `sqrt(log(2/δₖ)/(2n))`. | Union-bound control across three fixed ad sets and countably many predeclared looks, under independent stationary bounded trials. Conservative, potentially much slower. |

The seventh arm, **exact-binomial sequential reference**, applies one-sided binomial tail tests against break-even, each compared with `δₖ/2` at the same declared looks. It uses the same allocated familywise error budget under the Bernoulli assumptions and measures whether exact tails improve decision time over Hoeffding; no improvement is presumed before the run. This implements tail tests, not a new interval; [NIST's technical note](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.2119.pdf) describes the corresponding exact-binomial test/interval foundation. The provider corpus still uses the existing Hoeffding readiness gate; the exact arm is evaluation-only.

Each sequential arm's total error allocation is at most 0.05 because the allocation sums across both tails, three ad sets, and all looks. This applies separately to each prespecified procedure, not to opportunistically choosing whichever procedure first gives a desired answer. These guarantees permit optional stopping at **predeclared sample-size checkpoints**, with the original look count and candidate inventory retained. They do not cover arbitrary data-dependent sample sizes with an occasional-look index, uncounted extra peeks, or index resets. Independence between looks is unnecessary, but the underlying sampling assumptions must hold. A new ad-set family or changed data regime needs a new, explicit error-budget design; one must not repeatedly restart the 0.05 budget and advertise an account-wide guarantee. Neither method remedies biased attribution or adaptive changes to the conversion population.

Scenarios include genuine winners, all losers, near break-even rates, selected early lucky streaks, delayed outcomes with mature cohorts, and both detected and undetected rate drift. The early-luck-conditioned scenario intentionally changes the sampling distribution; the stationary unconditional guarantee cannot simply be claimed for that selected subset. Drift scenarios deliberately violate stable-rate assumptions. Publish failures and abstentions rather than dropping these cases.

Report campaign-level false-profit-claim rate with its campaign denominator and Monte Carlo uncertainty, ad-set false positive/negative claims, missed genuinely positive opportunities, first-decision clicks and spend, no-decision fraction, true-best selection, and posterior bins against simulator truth. Decision-time summaries exclude no-decision campaigns, so always show their coverage alongside speed. Posterior bins are descriptive over the chosen synthetic scenario mix, not real-world calibration. Repeated looks are not independent trials; whole campaigns are the simulation replication unit. A run can prove code behavior under these generated conditions, not production profitability or the earliest possible decision under all conditions.

### Earliest high confidence needs sequential error control

Checking conventional fixed-sample intervals repeatedly until one looks favourable changes their error rate. [Johari and colleagues](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.2135) develop always-valid A/B inference; [Howard and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240) develop time-uniform confidence sequences. These are alternatives for rigorous continuous monitoring, not features supplied by a Typesafe score. The allocated Hoeffding and exact-binomial arms are initial references, not implementations of those papers' more efficient procedures.

Many ad sets also create multiple-testing risk. For consequential recommendations, distinguish controlling the chance of **any** false discovery in a defined family from controlling the expected **fraction** of false discoveries. [Wang and Ramdas's e-BH procedure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02824) is a primary-source route to FDR control with valid e-values, including dependence; it is a future research candidate, **not implemented** here. A Jev confidence or Bayesian posterior must not be relabelled an e-value or always-valid p-value.

### Where Typesafe earns its place

Compare **numerical-only**, **the same numerical engine plus Typesafe semantic findings**, and **the same engine plus Gemini semantic findings** on identical frozen snapshots. The candidate value is catching an invalid applicability assumption, an offer/landing mismatch, mistaken funnel-event identity, or an unsupported profit narrative—not recalculating `c/m`. Semantic flags must not silently become new numeric priors or bypass source validation.

The first gain to establish is fewer unsafe confident recommendations at useful coverage and lower total review cost. Earliest useful decision is a trade-off curve—error, decision time, media exposure, and abstention—not “fastest response wins.” An abstaining classifier can look safe by never helping; a fast overconfident classifier can destroy value. Report both.

### Evidence gates for progressing beyond simulation

1. **Source validity:** exact ad-set hierarchy, currency, consistent event denominator, attributable observation basis, matured cohorts, deduplicated outcomes, and approved cost/margin definition are present. Missing items yield insufficient evidence; no guessed values.
2. **Numerical validity:** required GitHub checks verify the posterior/bound implementation and edge cases. Frozen seeded runs publish all arms/scenarios, error denominators, stopping times, and no-decision rates. Passing synthetic checks grants no production authority.
3. **Independent semantic utility:** blinded real-data labels establish where Typesafe catches invalid assumptions beyond numerical gates, versus Gemini and current review. Use campaign/time holdouts, predeclared risk tolerances and sample-size/power requirements; fit no thresholds to the final holdout.
4. **Calibration and robustness:** validate posterior predictions and semantic findings separately, including prior sensitivity, delayed refunds/conversions, missing events, tracking changes, product mix, and population drift. [Meta's own robustness work](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/07/10/data-infrastructure/machine-learning-ml-prediction-robustness-meta/) distinguishes label-data instability from calibration drift. A tracking outage is not evidence that an ad became unprofitable.
5. **Read-only prospective shadow:** preserve existing decisions; record first eligible recommendation and later matured outcomes. State counterfactual and survivorship limits. Require the predeclared safety/coverage/cost criteria with uncertainty, not a hand-picked success example.
6. **Explicitly authorized controlled rollout:** compare recommendation policies on appropriate independent units with stable approved spend, margin-qualified business outcomes, conversion follow-up, an error budget and loss stop. Until then, human approval remains required and no automatic budget changes are introduced. Any later profitable-campaign reduction or budget increase still follows Sal's explicit permission rules.

## Production gap and safe data path

The [existing integration](typesafe-operations.md) supports opted-in advertising recommendations, native evidence, and explicit status-action controls. This evaluation lane does **not** establish complete sibling discovery, validated mature historical windows, an empirically calibrated creative-ranking policy, or improved production advertising outcomes. Normal orchestration remains shadow-only. Current advertising authority and feature flags are unchanged.

The next read-only evaluation data path should be:

1. **Complete sibling inventory:** fetch all relevant individual ads under the exact account/campaign/ad-set hierarchy, including delivery/effective status, pagination completeness, creative identity, and observation timestamps. An incomplete list cannot establish the only winner. Ad IDs and creative-asset IDs are not interchangeable.
2. **Comparable history:** collect explicitly dated seven- and 30-day views where available, using the account timezone and the same objective, event mapping, currency, attribution settings, and reporting-time convention. These are evaluation windows, not a change to Sal's default reporting preference. Exclude or identify immature periods using observed conversion lag and pipeline status; no universal seven-day maturity assumption.
3. **Deterministic calculations:** compute spend, event counts, cost per event, link CPC, attributed revenue/ROAS, uncertainty, missingness, freshness, and business-target comparisons. Preserve unknown values. Do not substitute Meta leads for WebinarJam signups/attendance or attributed revenue for Stripe cash/net profit.
4. **Semantic review:** evaluate bounded creative/page evidence, recommendation support, and eligibility ambiguity. Return structured findings with a no-action/insufficient-evidence path. Content is untrusted data, not authorization.
5. **Existing approval:** expose exact ad-level recommendations for review through existing previews and human approval. No budget adjustment, last-active shutdown, or status change is authorized by this study. Source readback verifies an approved status change, not its profitability.

Meta's [official SDK](https://github.com/facebook/facebook-python-business-sdk/blob/main/facebook_business/adobjects/adsinsights.py) exposes ad-level IDs, attribution windows, report-time choices, metrics, and separate asset breakdowns. Their availability in the SDK is not a guarantee that every breakdown/metric combination is supported for an account. If asset-level conversion data is unavailable, report that limit rather than infer it from ad totals.

## How to prove benefit without fooling ourselves

**Same ad set is not a randomized experiment.** Meta describes [personalized per-impression ads ranking](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/08/05/ml-applications/from-user-sequences-to-scaling-laws-a-multi-stage-architecture-for-metas-ads-ranking/). Ads can receive different people, placements, timing, and delivery amounts. Low delivery is not evidence of poor creative; the lowest observed CPA is not automatically the best incremental ad. [Facebook-coauthored research](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/gordon_b/files/fb_comparison.pdf) found observational estimates often differed from randomized advertising experiments even with extensive adjustment.

- **Freeze decision-time evidence.** Store what was genuinely available then. A report fetched later can include conversions backfilled into earlier dates; feeding it to a historical decision leaks future information. Save reporting settings and snapshot/measurement times, not only date ranges.
- **Separate baseline types.** Compare against deterministic calculations/policy, the existing recommendation process, and the same provider without semantic enrichment. A deliberately weak keyword baseline is not the business-as-usual advertising policy. Show where ordinary code already wins at negligible inference cost.
- **Avoid post-treatment adjustment.** Spend, delivery, audience mix, and observed conversions after a pause or optimizer change are affected by that decision. Do not use them as pre-decision predictors or treat only surviving ads as an unbiased validation set. Retained ads do not reveal outcomes the paused ads would have produced.
- **Prevent creative leakage.** Split by time and creative/ad-set family; the same underlying creative in another crop, duplicate, or campaign must not leak between development and holdout. Keep outcome labels and future period summaries outside model inputs.
- **Validate semantic quality independently.** Prelabel representative authorized real cases, including uncertain ones; adjudicate disagreements without seeing provider identities. Measure dangerous false pauses/false acceptance, missed mismatches, coverage, and human review effort. Model confidence is not a calibrated probability of ad profit.
- **Measure the whole workflow.** Include retrieval, extraction/OCR/transcription, model calls, retries, reviewer corrections, and timing. Count failures and unknown billing. A cheaper typed judgment is not proof of cheaper advertising operations or lower media spend.
- **Reserve profit claims for an authorized experiment.** Historical replay and prospective no-action shadow can qualify safety and usefulness. Proving profit/qualified-conversion improvement needs a predeclared controlled policy comparison, stable authorized budgets, conversion-maturity follow-up, sufficient independent units, and loss guardrails. Agreement with another model is not that experiment.

The practical near-term target is a trustworthy per-ad review table: evidence-backed keep candidates, pause candidates, and explicit “not enough evidence,” with semantic issues surfaced cheaply. Moving from that table to execution remains a separate policy and approval decision.
